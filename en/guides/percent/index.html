Saving when your income
is never the same twice

Take a share out of every payment, the moment it lands, instead of waiting to see what is left at the end of the month. A good month saves more, a quiet month saves less. It never asks for money you have not been paid yet, which is the one reason it works for people whose income moves.

What the percentage method is

Instead of deciding you will save $500 a month, you decide you will save 10% of everything that comes in. Paid $2,000, save $200. Paid $6,000, save $600. However many times money arrives, that is how many times you split it.

The difference that matters is not the number. It is the timing — this method saves when the money arrives, not at the end of the month, because what is left at the end of the month usually is not left.

A fixed amount asks for money you may not have yet. A percentage asks for money already in your hand.

What percentage is the right one

The number you hear most is 10%, and it is a fine place to start. But the right percentage is the one you can hold for months in a row, not the one that looks good the day you set it.

Start at 5% if…You have never saved regularly before, or your income has only just settled. 5% is small enough not to change your life, and what we are building here is the habit, not the pile.
Set 10% if…The necessary bills are covered and there is still something left. It is big enough to show a result inside six months, and still small enough to keep up.
Go to 15–20% if…You have a goal with a real deadline, or your income has just gone up. Raise the percentage the moment the income rises and you will not feel anything go missing.

Do not start at a number you have never managed before. Starting at 20% and stopping in month two leaves you with less than starting at 5% and finishing the year.

Had this person set a fixed $1,000 a month instead, the quiet month breaks immediately — and by the research on quitting, the first miss is where most people stop. On a percentage, the quiet month still works. It just saves less. What matters is that they have not quit.

What it is good at, what it costs you, and who it suits

Good at
  • It never asks for money you have not been paid, so no payment is impossible
  • A good month saves more on its own, with no plan to rewrite
  • When your income rises, the amount rises the same day, with no decision to make
  • There are no empty boxes to see, because there are no scheduled payments
  • It saves at the moment money arrives, before you have attached it to anything
Costs you
  • A quiet month saves almost nothing
  • You can never know the total in advance, so you cannot say when you will reach a goal
  • No automatic transfer. You have to split it yourself every time
  • You have to know what you were actually paid each time. No record, no method
  • A flat percentage may not be enough when your income swings hard
Suits you if
  • You freelance, or take work by the job, and no two months match
  • You run a shop, where money comes in daily but never the same
  • You have several sources of income arriving at different times
  • You have set a fixed amount before and could not make it in a tight month
  • Your income is climbing
Not for you if
  • You need an exact sum by an exact date, like tuition
  • You want to set an automatic transfer and never think about it
  • You do not want to write down what you were paid
  • Your income is already very steady, in which case a fixed amount is simpler

What the research says

JPMorgan Chase Institute, October 2019
Anonymised account data from around 6 million families, followed month by month from 2012 to 2018.
36%
is how much the typical family’s month-to-month income moved
Against the previous year’s average
65%
of families held less cash than they needed
What they needed was around six weeks of income

An uneven income is not a minority problem. The typical family met large income swings in nearly five months of the year, and income spiked up about twice as often as it dropped. Planning on the assumption that every month brings the same amount is an assumption that does not match most people to begin with.

Thaler and Benartzi · the Save More Tomorrow program
Employees commit in advance to raising their savings rate when their pay rises, rather than deciding to raise it while their pay is unchanged.

At the first company to run it, people who joined raised their savings rate from 3.5% to 13.6% of pay in under four years, and 78% of those offered it joined.

Raise the percentage when the income rises. At that moment you are not used to the new money yet, so nothing feels lost.

Where the same research argues against this

I think a page that recommends a method should also say what the research it is quoting argues against.

BEFORE YOU USE THIS METHOD

Three things to know

  • A flat percentage can leave you under-prepared. The JPMorgan Chase researchers say so themselves, and recommend saving harder in the months income spikes. So in a busy month, do not stop at the usual percentage — put the extra away too.
  • Both of these are US data. The 36% and the six weeks come from American bank accounts, and Save More Tomorrow ran at American employers. For this page that is the right country — but if you are reading from elsewhere, the figures are not yours.
  • Save More Tomorrow is about salaried employees, which is the opposite of the people this page is for. I use only its lesson about raising the percentage when income rises. I do not borrow its numbers for freelancers.

Setting a goal when you cannot know the total

This is the flaw in the method that cannot be fixed — you cannot say when you will arrive. But it can be worked around, by changing what you measure.

Measure the percentage you kept, not the total you reachedThis month’s goal is “split 10% every time money came in”, which can be achieved in any month, whatever the income was.
Use your worst month as the floorIf the quietest month of last year brought in $2,500, then at 10% you will save at least $250 a month. Work out how many months that is to your target, and every month better than that is profit.
Review it every three monthsLook at what you actually saved, and only then say when you will arrive. That is more accurate than a guess made in month one.
